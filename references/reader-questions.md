# External-reader question bank

The governing principle: ask readers to report their **experience**, never to propose **solutions**. "What would you change?" turns a reader into an amateur editor and produces noise — most readers are unreliable diagnosticians of their own reactions even when the reactions themselves are accurate and worth having. Every question below is designed to surface a *reading effect* (see the evidence doctrine in `SKILL.md`) without inviting a *causal interpretation* in the same breath. Ask the effect question first; if a cause comes up unprompted, log it as a separate, lower-confidence claim — don't stop the reader from offering it, just don't treat it with the same weight as the effect itself.

## Core questions, in roughly this order

1. **Where did you start to lose interest?** (not: what was boring — the *where*, as precisely as they can locate it, is the data; whether they can say *why* is a separate, weaker signal.)
2. **What were you expecting to happen at [a specific point]?** (surfaces whether the text is setting up expectations it intends to, and whether payoffs land where the author thinks they do.)
3. **What did you understand about [character/event/relationship] by this point?** (tests whether information the text intends to convey actually arrived — ask about specific things you're unsure landed, not just generally.)
4. **When did you change your mind about [a character, a belief, a relationship]?** (tests whether a turn the text is built around is actually felt as a turn, and roughly where.)
5. **What felt artificial or engineered to you, anywhere?** (surfaces "machine-visible" moments directly, without asking the reader to guess at mechanism.)
6. **What question did you think the book was actually asking, as you were reading it?** (tests whether the book's real center of gravity — as opposed to its stated subject — is coming through; compare their answer to the Phase 0 contract.)

## Secondary / follow-up questions

Use these only after the core pass, and only to clarify an effect already reported — not to open new territory:

- Can you point to roughly where that started? (localizing an effect already named)
- Did that feeling change later, or stay the same to the end?
- Was there a moment you stopped believing a character would do something?
- Is there anything you expected to come back later that never did?

## What to avoid asking, and why

- **"What would you change?"** — invites solution-proposing, which is a different (and much less reliable) skill than experience-reporting. If a reader offers this unprompted, thank them and record it as a low-confidence causal interpretation, separate from any effect they also reported.
- **"Did you like it?"** — a global preference verdict, not a located signal. Almost never actionable on its own.
- **Leading questions that name a suspected defect** ("did chapter 8 feel repetitive?") — plants the diagnosis in the reader's mind before they've reported anything themselves. If a specific worry needs testing, phrase it as one of the core questions above (where did interest drop) and see if the reader's own answer converges there unprompted.

## Recording responses

Transcribe close to verbatim before synthesizing anything — see `registers.md` for the reader-response log format. Tag each piece of the response by evidence-doctrine category (reading effect vs. causal interpretation) as it's recorded, not after the fact during synthesis; retroactive tagging is where the effect/cause distinction quietly gets lost.

When two readers diverge on the same point, record both fully rather than averaging or picking the more articulate one — a genuine split is itself a finding (see Phase 8 in `phases.md`).
